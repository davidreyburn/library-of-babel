# The Library of Babel — Visual Design Specification

**Document ID:** LIB-BABEL-DESIGN-001
**Status:** Direction resolved (§6), consequences in §7, and §9 records where the built prototype has since departed from both
**Derived from:** Borges, "The Library of Babel" (1941) — not included in this repository, see [`SOURCE.md`](../SOURCE.md)
**Companion:** [`technical-specification.md`](./technical-specification.md)

---

## 1. The Sparseness Problem

Borges wrote roughly 2,900 words and spent almost none of them on appearance. There is no description of a wall's material, a book's binding, a floor, a ceiling colour, a sound, a smell, or a temperature. Two colours are named in the entire story. This document therefore does three things, kept strictly separate:

1. **§3 — What the text fixes.** Determinate visual requirements, quoted and traced. Small in number, but unusually specific where they exist.
2. **§4 — Mood and tone.** Not visual instruction, but the emotional register any visual treatment must land.
3. **§6 — Open slots (VIS-01 … VIS-13).** Everything the text leaves to taste, enumerated as numbered decisions rather than quietly invented. These are the questionnaire's subject.

The discipline that matters: **do not let §6 leak into §3.** Most existing visualisations of this story fail by treating invented atmosphere as though Borges had specified it. §5 catalogues the specific ways that goes wrong.

## 2. Summary of Available Visual Material

Exhaustively, the text supplies:

| Category | What is actually given |
|---|---|
| **Colour** | Black (the letters). Crimson (one hexagon). Nothing else. |
| **Light** | Two spherical lamps per hexagon, transversally placed; "insufficient, incessant." |
| **Geometry** | Hexagonal gallery; ceiling barely above bookcase height; narrow hallway; spiral stair; vast vertical shafts; very low railings. |
| **Surface** | The mirror is polished. The stairways are polished. Nothing else has a stated finish. |
| **Type** | Printed letters: punctual, delicate, perfectly black, inimitably symmetrical. Hand-written: crude, wavering. |
| **Condition** | Incorruptible, perfectly motionless. One broken stairway. |
| **Motion** | Wind, generated by an endless fall. Otherwise motionless. |
| **Figures** | Sparse to absent — nights of travel without meeting a single librarian. |

That is the whole inventory. Everything else in this document is either mood inference or a declared open slot.

## 3. Determinate Requirements

### 3.1 Light — the strongest directive in the story

- **D-01** Exactly two light sources per hexagon, spherical, **transversally placed** — i.e. opposed across the room, not centred. Lighting must therefore be **bilateral**, producing two overlapping pools with a measurable falloff between them, never a flat ambient wash.
- **D-02** Light is **insufficient**. Underlit is correct; comfortable is a defect. Reading in this room should look difficult.
- **D-03** Light is **incessant** — permanently on. There is **no day, no night, no dusk, no dawn, and no flicker.** Any temporal lighting cycle is a fabrication, and flicker in particular contradicts the word directly. Whatever unease the design carries must come from insufficiency, not instability.
- **D-04** The lamps are introduced as "spherical fruit which bear the name of lamps" — the story's only figurative description of a manufactured object, and it is **botanical**. The lamps should read as *grown* rather than engineered: no filaments, fixtures, cabling, sconces, or industrial hardware.

### 3.2 Colour

- **D-10** Printed letters are black — restated later as "perfectly black." This is the only absolute value in the story and should be the design's true black.
- **D-11** **Crimson is the sole named hue**, attached to a single unreachable hexagon of magical books. It is therefore the accent by authorial appointment, and it is structurally *rare* — one hexagon out of an unbounded lattice. A design that spreads crimson liberally destroys the only colour event Borges wrote.
- **D-12** Every other hue is unspecified. See VIS-01.

### 3.3 Proportion

- **D-20** Ceiling height equals shelf height and "scarcely exceeds that of a normal bookcase." The gallery is a **low, compressed room** — emphatically not a cathedral, atrium, or vaulted hall.
- **D-21** The shafts open the space **vertically without limit**; upper and lower floors are visible "interminably."
- **D-22** These two facts are in productive tension and both must be legible: **crushed horizontally, infinite vertically.** The room presses down on the occupant while the void beside it falls away forever. This tension is the story's central spatial idea and the single most important thing for a visual treatment to get right.
- **D-23** Railings are **very low** — the danger is casual and unguarded, not dramatised.

### 3.4 Typography — two registers, explicitly contrasted

Borges sets up a direct comparison, and it is the most designed passage in the story: the narrator's own marks against the letters inside the books.

- **D-30 Printed register:** *punctual, delicate, perfectly black, inimitably symmetrical.* High-contrast, precise, mechanically even, and — critically — **inimitable**: it must look unreproducible by hand.
- **D-31 Hand register:** *crude, wavering.* Applied by a fallible hand to book **covers**, never to pages.
- **D-32** The gap between these registers is the story's evidence for the distance between the divine and the human. **The two faces must be unmistakably different in kind**, not merely in weight or size. If a viewer could believe one produced the other, the passage's argument is lost.
- **D-33** Spine lettering exists and does **not** correspond to contents (see the technical spec, LIB-L-006). Spine text must therefore never look like a title. It should read as label, not name.

### 3.5 Condition — the counterintuitive requirement

- **D-40** The Library is **incorruptible** and **perfectly motionless**. The books do not decay, fade, foxen, or gather patina. The closing description is "illuminated, solitary, infinite, perfectly motionless, equipped with precious volumes, useless, incorruptible, secret."
- **D-41** **The Library is not a ruin.** This is the most commonly violated requirement in existing interpretations. The structure is pristine and permanent; the *inhabitants* are what has decayed.
- **D-42** Wear therefore belongs **only to human traces**: scrawled covers, one broken stairway, hidden metal disks in a forbidden dice cup, bodies committed to the shafts, shelves emptied by the Purifiers. Dust, cobwebs, rot, rust, and crumbling stone are all fabrications and contradict D-40.
- **D-43** The correct atmosphere is **abandonment without decay** — a place that will outlast everyone in it and shows no sign of trying. See VIS-06, which offers this as a choice rather than imposing it, since the fork is interpretively significant.

### 3.6 Composition

- **D-50** "The distribution of the galleries is invariable." Every gallery is identical. There is **no landmark, no hierarchy, no signage, no wayfinding, and no focal point.**
- **D-51** This is an instruction toward **anti-composition**: repetition without accent, symmetry without emphasis. Conventional visual interest — a hero view, a dominant diagonal, a figure placed at the third — is a departure from the text and should be a conscious one.
- **D-52** The classic dictum is that the Library is a sphere whose centre is any hexagon and whose circumference is inaccessible. Formally: **every position is the centre; no position is distinguished.** Placelessness is the goal.
- **D-53** Each hallway holds a mirror that "faithfully duplicates all appearances." Doubling is an available motif, and the text attaches genuine ambiguity to it — the men read it as proof of limits, the narrator as a promise of the infinite. The mirror should not resolve the question.

  **Built** (see LIB-BABEL-SPEC-001 §17.13 and §17.4). A real single-bounce reflection, shaded through the same path as the primary ray. It hangs in two places: in an alcove off a corridor, and — in 22% of reading rooms — alone on the wall of a room holding nothing else. Together, a mirror stands in about 1 cell in 80. *Faithfully* is taken literally: the loss at the surface is in level, not in hue. The ambiguity survives by arithmetic rather than by taste — one bounce means a facing pair doubles the corridor once and terminates in a dark pane, so the mirror shows neither a bounded room nor an endless one.

### 3.7 Air

- **D-60** The only motion in the story is **wind generated by an endless fall**. Air moves; nothing else does.

## 4. Mood and Tone

### 4.1 Register

The epigraph is from *The Anatomy of Melancholy*, so melancholy is licensed by the author. But the specific quality is **exhausted, administrative melancholy** — the narrator is a working librarian keeping records, not a romantic mourning a loss. He reports atrocity and futility in the same measured voice he uses for shelf counts. **The visual treatment should be composed, not anguished.**

### 4.2 The emotional arc

If the treatment is sequential — a scroll, a film, a game's progression — the story supplies a usable structure:

1. **Inventory.** Flat, architectural, factual. The narrator counts shelves.
2. **Totality proclaimed.** "Extravagant happiness"; the universe justified; hope at its widest.
3. **The pilgrimages.** Crowds, disputes, stranglings on the stairs, books flung into the shafts, madness.
4. **"Excessive depression."** The precious book is real, and permanently out of reach.
5. **Fanaticism.** The Purifiers; millions destroyed; the delirium of the Crimson Hexagon.
6. **The prayer.** The one lyric break — *let heaven exist, though my place be in hell.* Brief warmth, immediately withdrawn.
7. **Decline.** Epidemics, banditry, suicides; the species ending while the Library persists.
8. **"Elegant hope."** Cold consolation.

- **T-01** The ending is **not despair.** "My solitude is gladdened by this elegant hope" is a real, if chilly, consolation — an intellectual satisfaction found in the structure itself. A treatment that ends in bleakness or horror overshoots the final page.
- **T-02** Conversely, item 6 is the only warmth in the story and should be the treatment's only warm moment, if it has one.

### 4.3 Failing sight as a design constraint

- **T-10** The narrator states early that his eyes "can hardly decipher" his own writing. The story is narrated by someone who **cannot see well, in a room that is deliberately too dark.** Strain is thematic.
- **T-11** This does **not** license illegible design. It licenses depicted difficulty — text shown at the edge of readability *within* the image — while the artefact the viewer actually operates stays legible. Distinguish the Library's illegibility from the interface's. See VIS-03, which is where this trade-off gets decided.

### 4.4 Solitude

- **T-20** Population density was one librarian per three hexagons and has fallen; the narrator recalls travelling many nights without meeting anyone. The default state of any view is **empty.** Crowds appear only in the historical pilgrimage passage.

## 5. Anti-Requirements

A conforming design must avoid these, each of which contradicts a cited requirement:

| Common treatment | Violates |
|---|---|
| Grand vaulted cathedral library | D-20 — the ceiling barely clears a bookcase |
| Dusty, cobwebbed, crumbling ruin | D-40, D-41 — incorruptible, motionless |
| Warm, cosy, wood-and-brass reading room | D-02 — light is insufficient; T-20 — solitude |
| Candlelight, flicker, guttering flame | D-03 — incessant; D-04 — no fixtures |
| Day/night cycle, shafts of sunlight | D-03 — no temporal cycle; the Library has no exterior |
| Ornament, carving, signage, gilt | D-50 — invariable, unmarked |
| Crimson as a general accent | D-11 — one hexagon, unreachable |
| Foxed, yellowed, decaying books | D-40 — incorruptible |
| A dramatic hero composition | D-51, D-52 — anti-composition, placelessness |
| Legible titles on spines | D-33 — spines do not name contents |
| Endless *horizontal* vistas as the signature shot | D-11/D-20 — the chain is narrow; the infinity is **vertical** |

The last one deserves emphasis. Because only two of six walls are free, the floor plan is a **chain**, not an open plane (technical spec, LIB-A-011). The Library's famous vastness is *not* a wide horizontal panorama — it is a cramped corridor beside a bottomless shaft.

## 6. Resolved Direction

The thirteen open slots have been settled. They are restated here as determinate requirements (**V-01 … V-13**), carrying the same authority as §3. The `VIS-` identifiers are retained for provenance.

### 6.1 The direction in one line

> **An encyclopaedia plate of a place that is organic, dark, and worn smooth by hands no longer there.**

Botanical light in a stone throat, cut in line. The selections cohere around a specific and unobvious reading: the Library as a *living* thing rendered by an *analytic* medium. Sodium light and verdigris both pursue the "spherical fruit" of D-04; basalt and engraving hold that warmth at arm's length. The friction between those two impulses is the design, and it should not be smoothed away.

### 6.2 Resolved requirements

- **V-01 · Palette — Verdigris Damp.**
  `#8C9179` pale · `#5F6650` recessed · `#363A2E` mid · `#12140F` ink · crimson per V-09.
  A sickly yellow-green cast throughout. Chosen for its organic register; see E-01 for the role reassignment this forces, and E-02 for where the green is actually visible.

- **V-02 · Light — Sodium Bloom.**
  Two spheres, transversally placed per D-01. Emission `rgb(255, 196, 110)`; ambient floor ≈ 0.055 of peak; falloff radius ≈ 46% of room width per lamp; linear gamma (soft, wide, no hard terminator). Warm amber is the most botanical reading of D-04 and the friendliest to the "fruit" simile. Never flickers (D-03).

- **V-03 · Legibility — Uniformly Dim.**
  Image and interface share one low-light world; no bright chrome surrounding a dark picture. **Dimness is a matter of absolute luminance, not of contrast ratio** — the two are independent axes, and this requirement governs only the first. See E-07 for the contrast floor that keeps the artefact operable without reintroducing the bright-UI option this rejects.

- **V-04 · Material — Black Basalt.**
  Walls, shelves, floor, stair: dense dark volcanic stone, base `rgb(38, 38, 42)`, matte except where V-06 applies. Basalt absorbs the lamps and makes insufficient light look weaker still, which serves D-02 and compounds with V-03 (see E-07).

- **V-05 · Grain — Engraved Hatch.**
  Tone is carried entirely by line: hatch density and cross-hatch, no continuous-tone rendering, no photographic noise, no film grain.

- **V-06 · Wear — Touch Polish.**
  No decay of any kind (D-40 holds absolutely). Wear appears only as burnish: stair rails, shelf edges, and door jambs polished by centuries of hands. Directly text-supported — the story twice describes the stairways as *polished*.

- **V-07 · Printed register — Old-Style Serif.**
  Humanist, even, moderate contrast, quietly authoritative. Reads as printed by a patient machine. Satisfies *punctual, delicate, perfectly black* (D-30).

- **V-08 · Hand register — Nervous Script.**
  Cursive, uneven, hurried — a hand that knows it is failing (T-10). Covers only, never pages (D-31). See E-05: in a line medium this contrast needs deliberate protection.

- **V-09 · Crimson — One Appearance.**
  **`#9E1B32`**, occurring **exactly once** across the entire body of work, as the Crimson Hexagon. Not an accent, not a system colour, not a UI state. Once spent it cannot be spent again. See E-06 for the production consequence and E-11 for a value conflict resolved.

- **V-10 · Mode — Engraving.**
  Line and hatch, no photographic optics, no lens artefacts, no depth-of-field. Depiction by mark, not by light transport.

- **V-11 · Framing — Held in Tension.**
  Compressed room and bottomless shaft in a single frame, per D-22. This is the hardest composition in the set and it constrains aspect ratio: the frame must give a low horizontal band to the gallery and full frame height to the shaft, so **portrait or square, never letterbox**. Letterboxing would deliver the horizontal crush at the cost of the vertical infinity, collapsing the tension this requirement exists to hold.

- **V-12 · Figures — Traces Only.**
  No bodies, near or distant. Evidence only: a scrawled cover, an abandoned lamp, a dice cup left in a latrine. Unifies with V-06 — see E-09.

- **V-13 · Motion — Air Only.**
  Architecture perfectly motionless (D-40). The sole moving elements are dust and page-edges stirred by the wind of the endless fall (D-60). See E-08 for how motion survives a static medium.

### 6.3 Still out of scope

Sound, smell, temperature, and the appearance of food or water remain unaddressed — the last of these is recorded as an unresolved gap in the technical specification at LIB-O-010, and no visual decision can close it.

## 7. Emergent Requirements

The thirteen selections interact. These requirements follow from *combinations* and are stated by no single choice; three resolve outright conflicts, and one is a risk that needs a hard number attached. They are binding.

### 7.1 Conflicts resolved

- **E-01 · Palette roles must be reassigned.**
  V-01 supplies a pale olive `#8C9179` as its ground value, but V-04 makes every architectural surface near-black basalt. As written the palette's lightest value has nothing to attach to. **Resolution:** reassign by substance rather than by depth. `#8C9179` and `#5F6650` belong to **paper and book spines**; `#363A2E` and `#12140F` belong to **stone**. The consequence is exact and worth stating plainly: *the only pale objects in the Library are the books.* The room absorbs; the volumes hold what little light there is. This is a stronger reading than the palette alone implied and should be treated as the governing one.

- **E-02 · The green survives only in the falloff.**
  Amber emission (V-02) on olive-green surfaces (V-01) desaturates toward muddy neutral wherever the light is strongest. The verdigris cast will therefore read **only in the penumbra and the shadow**, not in the lit pools. Do not correct for this. The palette's identity living exactly where the light fails is a precise visual restatement of D-02, and it means the two lamps carve *colour* out of the room as well as brightness.

- **E-03 · Falloff must be tonally quantised.**
  V-02 specifies a soft, wide, linear falloff; V-05 and V-10 specify a pure line medium. Smooth continuous gradient is precisely what hatch renders worst — it bands, or it degenerates into mush. **Resolution:** quantise each lamp's falloff into **five to seven discrete tonal registers**, each a distinct hatch density, in the manner of an actual engraved plate. Do not attempt continuous gradation. This is both the historically correct technique and thematically apt: light in the Library becomes countable, discrete, and systematic, like everything else in it.

### 7.2 Consequences

- **E-04 · Hatch direction encodes the two lamps.**
  Because tone is line (V-05) and lighting is bilateral (D-01), hatch angle is available as a carrier of information. Run each region's primary hatch perpendicular to the incident direction of its nearer lamp, and let the two hatch fields cross in the contested zone between them. This follows classical practice — hatch follows form — and makes the *transverse* placement of D-01 legible in a medium that has no light transport of its own.

- **E-05 · The two type registers need protecting.**
  D-32 requires that printed and hand lettering differ *in kind*. But in an engraving everything is made of the same marks, which threatens to collapse V-07 into V-08. **Resolution:** distinguish them by engraving convention rather than by typeface alone. The printed register is **burin lettering** — ruled, mechanically even, closed forms. The hand register is **free etched line** — needle-drawn, open, variable in weight. Historical plates already separate formal engraved lettering from etched drawing; use that separation. Typeface choice alone will not carry D-32 in this medium.

- **E-06 · Crimson is a second plate.**
  A single crimson event (V-09) in a monochrome line medium (V-10) is, in production terms, a **spot colour requiring its own printing plate** — used once, for one image, and never again. Adopt this literally. The method then enforces the rarity rather than relying on restraint: crimson cannot leak into general use, because using it means pulling a second plate. This is the neatest alignment in the whole set, and it makes the Crimson Hexagon's unreachability a fact about the artefact and not only about the fiction.

- **E-08 · Motion must not betray the medium.**
  V-13 grants moving air; V-10 is a medium that does not move. Smooth interpolated animation would read as digital and dissolve the engraving. **Resolution:** if the treatment animates, run the moving marks at **roughly 8–12 frames per second** with no motion blur and no sub-pixel interpolation, so the result reads as successive plates rather than as video. Only dust and page-edges may move; every architectural line stays fixed frame to frame.

- **E-09 · The burnish *is* the trace.**
  V-06 and V-12 collapse into one directive. Polished rails and shelf edges are not merely a material property — they are the human evidence that V-12 permits. No bodies; the stone remembers the hands. Render this in the engraving as **reserved white**: burnished highlights are areas of *unhatched* ground, the brightest values in the plate and the only places where the paper shows through untouched.

- **E-10 · Precedent is now doubly indicated.**
  Engraving (V-10) plus held-in-tension framing (V-11) converge on Piranesi's *Carceri d'invenzione* — line-medium plates holding oppressive architecture against impossible vertical depth. Treat these as the primary reference for composition and hatch handling, with one correction: Piranesi's spaces are vaulted and grand, whereas D-20 caps the ceiling at bookcase height. Take the vertical logic and the mark-making; reject the monumentality.

- **E-11 · One crimson, not two.**
  V-01 arrives carrying `#96202E` as its bundled accent while V-09 specifies `#9E1B32`. **`#9E1B32` governs; `#96202E` is superseded and must not appear.** Two near-identical crimsons in a work whose entire colour argument rests on a single unrepeatable event would be a straightforward defect.

### 7.3 Risk requiring a number

- **E-07 · Three selections compound toward illegibility.**
  V-02 sets a low ambient floor (~0.055), V-03 dims the entire surface including the interface, and V-04 makes every surface a near-black stone that absorbs what light there is. Each is defensible; together they push hard toward unreadable, and the risk is worth naming rather than discovering in production.

  The resolution is the axis distinction in V-03: **dim is not the same as low-contrast.** A near-black ground carrying a pale olive foreground is very dark in absolute luminance while retaining an excellent contrast ratio. Accordingly:

  | Element | Requirement |
  |---|---|
  | Functional text — labels, controls, captions, anything actionable | **≥ 4.5:1** against its immediate ground |
  | Non-text interactive affordances and focus states | **≥ 3:1** |
  | Overall surface luminance | Unconstrained — go as dark as the direction wants |
  | *Depicted* text — letters shown inside the image, on pages and spines | **Exempt.** May fall to the threshold of legibility and below, per T-10 |

  This preserves the immersion V-03 was chosen for while keeping the artefact operable. It does not reintroduce the bright-chrome option V-03 rejected: the whole surface stays dark: only the *ratios* within that darkness are held. The one place I would push back is if functional text is pushed under the floor for atmosphere's sake; that trades a real cost for an effect the depicted text can deliver for free.

## 9. As Built — Supersessions

The Phase 1 prototype has moved on from parts of §6 and §7. Where the two
disagree, this section governs the prototype; §6 remains the record of what
was chosen and why, which is worth keeping legible even where it was later
overturned.

### 9.1 Rendering

**V-05 and V-10 are superseded.** Engraved hatch is gone; the image is
quantised to a short tone ramp with a dither. Three consequences of the line
medium go with it: **E-03** (falloff quantised into hatch registers),
**E-04** (hatch angle following the lamp) and **E-05** (burin lettering
against free etched line) no longer apply, since nothing is drawn in line.

The dither is selectable at run time — ordered (8×8 Bayer), noise
(interleaved gradient noise, the default), fine (10 levels at lower
amplitude), or off. IGN was chosen because an ordered matrix leaves a
visible weave on any flat wall; a hash scatters at the same cost.

**E-09 survives in spirit.** Burnish is still reserved white — the top tone
step rather than unhatched ground.

Two artefacts had to be handled and are worth recording, because they look
alike and are not:

- *Dither moiré*: an 8×8 pattern resampled by a non-integer factor beats
  against the output grid. Fixed by rendering at integer divisions of the
  canvas with nearest-neighbour upscaling.
- *Geometric aliasing*: 35 spines across a 1.82 m wall fall below one pixel
  at a few metres, and their per-volume colours beat. Fixed by converging
  per-volume detail on its mean with distance — the procedural equivalent
  of a mip level. Supersampling also fixed it and was withdrawn: four times
  the pixels was not worth it.

### 9.2 Palette and surface

**E-01 extended.** The reassignment (pale to paper, dark to stone) now also
gives the floor and all casework a warmer wood, and upholstery a dark hide.
The verdigris still survives only in the falloff (**E-02** holds).

**Floors are boards, not tiles**: 19 cm planks running one way with butt
joints staggered board to board, and tone between them. Seams in both axes
read as tile, which is the wrong material entirely.

**Spines** are one flat colour per volume, keyed to the volume's own hash
rather than to hit position — keying it to height striped every spine in
register. Wear is what a spine actually gets: darkening at head and tail,
and a paler label band on some books. Random blotching reads as camouflage
in a monochrome palette and was removed.

**V-09 holds.** The crimson still appears exactly once, at one address.

### 9.3 Light

**V-02 holds** in character — two lamps per gallery, transversally placed,
insufficient and incessant — with three additions the space needed:

- Stairwell lamps climb with the flight. Fixed at a storey height they sat
  above the sloping ceiling, outside the corridor, and a downward-facing
  ceiling then takes `dot(n, L) < 0` and renders black.
- Shafts have no lamps of their own, but gather a weak fill: they are the
  one place with sightlines up and down many storeys, and without it the
  well is unreadable.
- A reading room's lamp is a real source, and its shade renders emissive.

### 9.4 Furniture — outside the text entirely

§5 of this document lists eleven treatments a faithful design must avoid.
The reading room is a twelfth, taken deliberately: Borges gives his
librarians shelves, a latrine and somewhere to sleep standing up. A recliner
is an intrusion, and is meant to read as one — rare (about 1 cell in 48),
and the only place in the Library with no books at all.

### 9.5 What has not moved

**V-01** Verdigris Damp, **V-03** uniformly dim, **V-04** dark stone,
**V-06** wear only as burnish and human trace, **V-07/V-08** the two type
